# Attribution and data sources

LUNAVI is an independent project for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026. It uses publicly available NASA data and services. It is not a NASA product and is not affiliated with, approved or endorsed by NASA, JPL, GSFC or Arizona State University. No NASA insignia or logotype is used.

## Data used by the science pipeline

**LOLA south-pole site DEMs: PGDA product 78** (5 m/pixel; MOON_ME frame, DE421)
- Source: NASA GSFC Planetary Geodynamics Data Archive, https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/78
- Citation: Barker, M. K., Mazarico, E., Neumann, G. A., Smith, D. E., Zuber, M. T., & Head, J. W. (2021). Improved LOLA elevation maps for south pole landing sites: Error estimates and their impact on illumination conditions. *Planetary and Space Science*, 203, 105119. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pss.2020.105119

**LOLA regional south-pole DEMs: PGDA product 90** (cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs; MOON_ME frame, DE421)
- Source: https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/90
- Citation: Barker, M. K., Mazarico, E., Neumann, G. A., Smith, D. E., Zuber, M. T., Head, J. W., & Sun, X. (2023). A New View of the Lunar South Pole from the Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter (LOLA). *The Planetary Science Journal*, 4, 183. https://doi.org/10.3847/PSJ/acf3e1

**Site numbering and the Earth-visibility criterion** (the PGDA product 69 illumination study)
- Source: https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/69
- Citation: Mazarico, E., Neumann, G. A., Smith, D. E., Zuber, M. T., & Torrence, M. H. (2011). Illumination conditions of the lunar polar regions using LOLA topography. *Icarus*, 211(2), 1066–1081. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2010.10.030

**SPICE kernels from NASA JPL's Navigation and Ancillary Information Facility (NAIF)**
- Source: https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/naif/data_generic.html
- Kernels: `naif0012.tls`, `de421.bsp`, `moon_pa_de421_1900-2050.bpc`, `moon_080317.tf`, `moon_assoc_me.tf`
- Citations:
  - Acton, C. H. (1996). Ancillary data services of NASA's Navigation and Ancillary Information Facility. *Planetary and Space Science*, 44(1), 65–70.
  - Folkner, W. M., Williams, J. G., & Boggs, D. H. (2009). The Planetary and Lunar Ephemeris DE 421. *IPN Progress Report* 42-178.

**JPL Horizons** (optional ephemeris cross-check)
- Source: NASA JPL Solar System Dynamics, https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html
- Citation: Giorgini, J. D., et al. (1996). JPL's On-Line Solar System Data Service. *Bulletin of the American Astronomical Society*, 28(3), 1158.

**Optional site-coordinate source**
- Wueller et al. (2026), *Journal of Geophysical Research: Planets*, 131, e2025JE009434, and its dataset (CC BY 4.0).
- Not bundled. Cite it if you take coordinates from its `Landing_Sites` shapefile.

## Bundled with the app

| Asset | Origin |
|---|---|
| `illustrative_south_pole_terrain.jpg` | Procedurally generated by `Tools/generate_illustrative_assets.py`. Not NASA data, not georeferenced |
| App icon and logo mark | Original vector artwork generated by the same script. No SF Symbols or NASA marks |
| `DemoMission.json`, `DemoSites.json`, `DemoVisibility.json` | Hand-authored simulated values, labelled SIMULATED DEMO in the app. Real site names appear only as catalogue entries, citing PGDA product 78 |
| `ComputedScenario.json`, `computed_south_pole_basemap.jpg` (when added) | Derived by the LUNAVI pipeline from the LOLA products above. Credit those products when you show them |

No LROC imagery is bundled. If you add LROC images, credit **NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University** and take them from the PDS archive.
